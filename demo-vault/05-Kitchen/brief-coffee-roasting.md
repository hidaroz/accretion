## The roast

Green beans keep for a year; roasted beans are good for about two weeks. That asymmetry is
the entire argument for roasting at home.

A hot air roaster moves 100–150g at a time. The stages:

- **Drying**, 4–6 minutes. Moisture leaves, the beans go from green to yellow, and it smells
  like hay.
- **Maillard**, a few minutes. Browning, and the smell turns to toast.
- **First crack**, an audible snap like popcorn. This is the landmark that matters — the
  bean's structure fractures as steam escapes.
- **Development**, everything after first crack. Roast level is decided here.

Colour is a bad guide. Kitchen lighting is warm, beans darken for a while after they leave
the roaster, and the eye adapts. Time after first crack is a measurement; colour is an
impression.

## Levels

- **Light** — stop 30–60s after first crack. Acidic, floral, origin character intact.
- **Medium** — 90–120s. Balanced, more body, less acidity.
- **Dark** — into second crack. Roast flavour dominates and origin character is mostly gone.

Second crack is sharper and quieter than first, and it comes fast. Overshooting it by thirty
seconds is the difference between dark roast and ash.

## Resting

Roasted beans off-gas CO₂ for several days, and CO₂ interferes with extraction. Espresso
wants 7–14 days of rest; filter is drinkable sooner, around 3–5 days.

Airtight jar, room temperature, out of light. Not the freezer.
