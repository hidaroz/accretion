> TL;DR: A flour-and-water culture fed on a schedule. Dough timing is governed by temperature, not by the clock, which is why recipes disagree with each other and all of them are right somewhere.

## Starter

Equal weights flour and water, left out, fed daily. It smells like paste for a few days, then
briefly like acetone, then like yogurt when it is working. Whole rye gets it going faster than
white flour.

Maintenance is a 1:5:5 feed — one part starter, five flour, five water — once a day at room
temperature, or once a week in the fridge. A starter that has been neglected for a month is
usually recoverable with two or three feeds; the layer of dark liquid on top is alcohol, not
death.

Readiness: it should roughly double and dome within 4–8 hours of feeding. Use it just before
it peaks, not after it collapses.

## Temperature is the real variable

Every recipe gives times. Times are a proxy for temperature and they are only correct in the
kitchen the recipe was written in.

At 26°C bulk fermentation might be four hours. At 20°C the same dough needs seven or eight.
The dough does not know what time it is. Judge it by rise and feel — 30–50% volume increase,
domed surface, jiggle rather than slosh — and treat any stated time as an estimate.

This is the one thing that makes sourdough seem unpredictable to people who follow recipes
exactly, and it stops being unpredictable the moment you own a thermometer.

## Schedule

Evening feed, overnight rise. Morning: mix flour and water, autolyse an hour, add starter and
salt. Bulk ferment with folds every 30 minutes for the first two hours. Shape, then cold
retard in the fridge overnight — this develops flavour and makes a sticky dough manageable.

Bake from cold, in a preheated dutch oven: 20 minutes covered at 250°C, 20 minutes uncovered
at 220°C.

## What goes wrong

Dense and gummy means underproofed, or sliced while warm. Flat and spreading means
overproofed, or shaped too loosely. No ear means the scoring was too shallow or the angle too
steep — score at 30° or so, decisively, one confident cut.
